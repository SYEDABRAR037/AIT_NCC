## Configuration placement and versioning

### Versioned database policy

The immutable `BiometricChallengePolicy` should contain only approved, challenge-store operational values:

- `maxChallengeTtlMs`
- `globalOutstandingLimit`, `subjectOutstandingLimit`, `retainedLimit`
- `globalTokenCapacity`, `globalRefillPerSecond`, `subjectTokenCapacity`, `subjectRefillPerSecond`
- `consumedRetentionMs`, `expiredRetentionMs`
- `cleanupBatchLimit`, `cleanupSubjectLimit`

All listed operational values and scopes in Decisions 6a–6i and 7B are recorded as project-owner approved. This approval does not activate a policy or authorize a migration. Do not seed or activate a policy until the policy lifecycle audit and two-person approval path are implemented and verified. No row or zero/invalid values may be replaced by compiled constants. Missing or invalid policy, quota, or integrity-gate state means issue, consume, and cleanup fail closed.

Attendance-window governance is separate from challenge quota policy: store absolute start/end instants and the session’s IANA zone on each TrainingSession. `Asia/Kolkata`, the strict `[start,end)` interval, and no grace period are approved. Require `attendanceWindowEndAt > attendanceWindowStartAt`; allow a late start only when current time is at or after the scheduled start and strictly before the scheduled end; and reject biometric windows longer than **12 hours**. Integrity-event retention is indefinitely approved for integrity-gate transition events; do not reuse challenge metadata retention for those events.

Deployment configuration should contain environment-specific non-policy controls: PostgreSQL connection/pool settings and the approved connection-pool acquisition budget (1 second), `lock_timeout` (2 seconds), `statement_timeout` (5 seconds), and `idle_in_transaction_session_timeout` (10 seconds); approved challenge transaction/handler budgets (8/12 seconds), cleanup transaction/job budgets (15/20 seconds), reconciliation transaction/job budgets (25/30 seconds), and cleanup cadence/lag alerts (every 5 minutes; 15/60 minutes). Version deployment changes through change management without secrets in source control. They must not override or silently substitute for database policy. The biometric gate and feature flag remain disabled until all required policy settings are resolved, tested, and explicitly activated.

### Policy change protocol

1. Publish a canonical policy document/configuration with a unique version, effective date, approving authority, rationale, and stable canonical serialization.
2. Compute SHA-256 over the canonical content and store the immutable `policyVersion`/`policyHash`; reject edits to existing versions.
3. Record proposal, approval, rejection, activation, and deactivation in append-only policy lifecycle events retained indefinitely. Each event records policy version/hash, action, database timestamp, actor identity and durable-session reference, proposer, approver references, and applicable reason. Activation/deactivation also records previous/new policy versions and relevant approval reference. The current integrity-event model logs gate transitions but does not, by itself, record this policy lifecycle; P0.3 SQL/design must add these events or explicitly extend the audit model before activation. Runtime has no direct update/delete permission, and missing audit evidence fails closed.
4. Under maintenance locks, block the integrity gate, insert the new policy version, switch the global quota’s active version atomically, reconcile counters/bucket state, and record old/new versions and the approval reference.
5. Keep the gate BLOCKED until a separately authorized clean reconciliation/recovery succeeds. Do not automatically restore READY solely because a new version was inserted.
6. Retain old policy versions while challenges, events, or audit references depend on them; policy deletion remains restricted. Never rewrite old policy content or hashes.
